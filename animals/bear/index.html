The Bear

The bear is never alone. Every bear on the meadow is a mother with her cub walking at her left side, and everything she does is about that cub. She eats grass when she can and meat when she has to, but anything that comes too close to her cub goes to the top of the list. The cub, for its part, is scared of almost everything. It jumps at rabbits. It is still only about a third as jumpy as the deer, which tells you a lot about the deer.

Place on the food chain
Step 9. Two wolves make a bear. Two bears make a lion.
Eats
Grass, rabbits and deer, in that order of preference. The exception is an animal standing on her left, next to the cub. She eats that one first.
Eaten by
Lions and tigers. The elephant will also eat any bear, since every bear is one you raised.
Appetite
Patient, like the wolf. Protecting the cub does not make her hungrier. It only changes what she picks when she does eat.

A mother first

Left to herself, the bear is a gentle eater. When she gets hungry she would rather have a patch of grass than chase anything. If there is no grass beside her, she takes a rabbit, then a deer.

The cub changes that. It always walks on her left, and she watches that side closely. When she gets hungry, a rabbit or deer standing on her left is the first thing she eats, before the grass and before anything on her other sides. It doesn't matter whether you built that animal or it came from your hand. Grass on her left is fine. Grass never scared anybody.

The nervous cub

The cub is timid. When any animal stands on its side of the board, it starts to jump with little shock marks around it, on and off. The mother isn't scared, so only the cub shakes. On the board, that is your warning: whatever is making the cub nervous is the next thing the mother eats once she gets hungry.

As nervous as it is, the cub is a calm animal next to the deer. A frightened deer shakes about three times as much and three times as long. The cub has its mother. The deer has nobody.

The plan: two bears make a lion

  • Build it from wolves. Two wolves side by side make a bear. Your hand never deals one, so every bear is built.
  • Keep the left side clear. Don't park an animal you need on the bear's left. Put grass there, or nothing.
  • Use the left side on purpose. A dealt rabbit on her left is a meal you choose for her. It keeps her away from the deer you are building on her other sides.
  • Watch the cub. If the cub is jumping, something is standing on its side. Move your plans around it before the mother gets hungry.
  • Mind the big cats. Lions and tigers eat bears. A bear next to a hungry lion is in trouble, cub or no cub.
  • Pair it up for a lion. Two bears merge into a lion. Build the second one right beside the first.

Real bears

A mother bear protecting her cubs is one of the most dangerous animals in the forest. Many bear attacks on people happen because someone came between a mother and her cubs without knowing it. Cubs stay with their mother for one to three years and learn everything from her: what to eat, where to find it, and when to climb a tree to get out of trouble. Even big adult male bears tend to keep away from a mother with cubs. And bears really do eat mostly plants. Grass, berries, roots and nuts make up most of a black bear's diet, with meat when the chance comes.
