The Zebra

The zebra is the meadow's undisputed fashion icon. It never goes anywhere without its golden scarf, and it never stops sparkling. Every so often it sparkles so hard that there is an audible shing! and it strikes its best pose for a camera that isn't there. The zebra knows exactly how good it looks. Its goal is to be the first animal ever to walk at Paris Fashion Week.

Place on the food chain
Step 6. Two deer make a zebra. Two zebras make a buffalo.
Eats
Grass and sprouts. Strictly plant-based, darling.
Eaten by
Wolves, lions and tigers. The elephant will also eat any zebra, since every zebra is one you raised.
Appetite
Calm. It can go longer without food than the deer.

Made, never dealt

The zebra is the first animal your hand will never give you. Sprouts, grass, rabbits, foxes and deer can all arrive as a tile, but a zebra only exists if you build it: two deer, side by side. The zebra would like you to know it was not found lying around. It was made.

That also makes every zebra a raised animal, which matters later. An elephant eats only what you grew by merging, and to an elephant a zebra is a very good meal.

The scarf is the signal

On a crowded board, look for the gold. The scarf is the brightest colour anywhere on this part of the food chain, so a zebra is easy to spot. It also tells you how the zebra is doing. When it gets hungry, the knot comes undone, the ends hang down to its knees and the gold goes dull. A zebra would never be seen like that on purpose. Get it some grass.

It is a calm eater, at least. It can go longer between meals than the deer, and it wants nothing but grass and sprouts. Leave it with no plants beside it for too long, though, and it starves and leaves bones behind.

The plan: two zebras make a buffalo

  • Build it from deer. Line up two deer and they merge into a zebra. A chain of foxes beside deer beside a zebra can climb several steps in one tap.
  • Make the second one nearby. Since a zebra can't come from your hand, the next zebra has to be built too. Grow your second pair of deer right next to the first zebra so the merge lands where you want it.
  • Keep the wolves busy. A wolf would rather eat an animal from your hand than one you built. A dealt rabbit or fox beside a hungry wolf keeps your zebra off the menu.
  • Watch the scarf. If it starts coming undone, the zebra is hungry. Put grass beside it before you do anything else.

Real zebras

No two zebras have the same stripes. Each pattern is as individual as a fingerprint, so this zebra's sense of personal style is, in a way, accurate. Scientists still debate what the stripes are for, but one strong idea is that they confuse biting flies, which land on striped coats far less often than on plain ones. Zebras live in herds, spend most of the day grazing tough grass, and keep an eye out for lions and hyenas. As far as anyone knows, none of them has walked a runway yet.
