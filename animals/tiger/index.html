The Tiger

The tiger has the hardest face on the meadow: a cold, half-closed stare that never changes, and a ring of gold that flares when it gets hungry. It almost never comes out onto the grassland, so the other animals don't know it well. They give it a lot of room. That is a shame, because the tiger is a gentle soul. It is serious, it is quiet, and, though it would never say so, it is very fond of grass.

The hardest face on the meadow

It doesn't smile. It doesn't growl either. It just looks at you, half asleep and completely unimpressed, with gold spikes standing around its head. When it gets hungry, the spikes reach out even further. The tiger is rare, too. You have to build two lions to see one, so most of the meadow has only heard stories about it, and the stories are not kind.

Soft on grass

Here is what the stories leave out. When the tiger gets hungry, the first thing it looks for is grass. A patch of grass beside it is its favourite meal, and it eats that before any animal, even a cheap fox from your hand. It is not that it can't hunt. It is the top hunter below the elephant, and it can eat anything from a fox to a lion. It just doesn't see the point, when there is perfectly good grass right there.

On the board, put grass beside a tiger and its red frame lands on the grass, not on your animals. The face does not change at all. That is the tiger.

The plan: two tigers make an elephant

  • Build it from lions. Two lions side by side make a tiger. Your hand never deals one, so every tiger is built.
  • Keep grass beside it. A tiger with grass next to it eats the grass and leaves your animals alone. It is the cheapest way to keep the top of your food chain fed.
  • Watch the red frame. If it is on the grass, your animals are safe from this tiger. If it is on an animal, that one is the next meal.
  • Pair it up for an elephant. Two tigers merge into the elephant, the top of the food chain. Build the second one right beside the first.

Real tigers

Real tigers really are loners. Unlike lions, they live and hunt alone, keep to thick forest, and are rarely seen even where they live, which is a big part of why people find them so frightening. They are the largest of all the big cats. And while tigers are meat eaters through and through, they have been seen eating grass and other plants now and then, which may help their stomachs. So the soft spot is not entirely made up.
